\section*{Job Market Paper}
\begin{cvbody}
\textbf{Noncompete Agreements and the Allocation of Human Capital Within Firms}\\
(solo-authored)

Award: \textit{Semifinalist for Best Paper Award in Corporate Finance, FMA 2026}

Presentations: \textit{Tinbergen Institute Jamboree, ESE Cheap Thrills, Spanish Finance Forum PhD Day, 7th RCF-ECGI Conference, FMA 2026, RSM PhD Seminar 2026}

Noncompete agreements (NCAs) make it harder for workers to leave, but also
harder for firms to hire. I combine employee career histories from Revelio Labs with
staggered state-level increases in noncompete agreement (NCA) enforceability. I find
that stronger enforceability increases internal promotions and occupational reallocation. These adjustments are particularly pronounced among knowledge workers and
employees in operations- and product-related functions. At the firm level, knowledge
capital investment rises, but sales per employee fall, especially in labor-intensive firms. Together, my results suggest that restrictions on external mobility shift allocation toward internal labor market at the cost of labor productivity.
\end{cvbody}
